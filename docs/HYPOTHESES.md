## 1. The state at #11: no domain hypothesis

DESIGN §4.1 (frozen, owner-approved) named one hypothesis: the ECDSA equation
`ecdsaVerify (Q : Point) (z r s : ℕ) : Bool` with the promise
`ecdsaVerify Q z r s = true ↔ 0 < r, s < n ∧ (u₁·G + u₂·Q ≠ O) ∧ (u₁·G + u₂·Q).x mod n = r`,
to be an axiom with a trusted secp256k1 link in the evaluator.

#11 found the equation **definable and executable in Lean** and wrote it as a
definition: `lean/BsvScript/Crypto/Secp256k1.lean` (`ecdsaEquation`, over the
field `F_p` on `Nat`, Jacobian points, double-and-add scalar multiplication,
the square root for point decoding, the lax DER parser, the normalization of
`s`). The point decoding, the lax DER parser, the normalization and the equation
are transcribed from the vendored libsecp256k1 and `pubkey.cpp` at the pin with
their lines; the field and group arithmetic computes the same values by other means
(Fermat inverses: the scalar's for the reference's `secp256k1_scalar_inverse_var`,
`ecdsa_impl.h:208`, and the field's in `toAffine`, which the reference's verification
does not do, comparing `R.x` in Jacobian coordinates instead, `ecdsa_impl.h:233–248`; the
square root by exponentiation, the textbook doubling, double-and-add; the module's header
lists each against the reference's function), and
the tests below are the evidence that the values agree. So `lean/AXIOMS-ALLOWED` is
unchanged: **the spec carries no axiom beyond Lean's three**, and every theorem of
`lean/AxiomCheck.lean` is axiom-clean in that sense (53 at #10; the list has grown with
every family since, its length at each run in that run's record; the three theorem
counts and their instruments are `spec/README.md`'s sentence).

## 3. What remains outside Lean

The harness (`harness/`, Rust): bsv-rs 0.3.32's verdicts and signatures since 2026-10-06 (R1.6; 0.3.24's
at #11, 0.3.28's before #47, 0.3.30's from #47, 2026-09-27; the pin moves are `docs/PROVENANCE.md` §1), the two SDKs' and GoBDK's
verdicts, and the node oracle's (`harness/oracle/`, the spec's own SV-node
instances). They are the judges of the spec, never part of it.

**The compiler is trusted, and named (2026-09-27; the direction audit's item (b), on the parent's paste of
17:15 UTC; re-cut 2026-09-29 after lean #32's second lens, findings A-1, A-6, A-7, A-9, A-13, A-14).** What
the kernel checks is the definitions, the theorems, and each `@[csimp]` lemma's statement (that a fast
implementation equals its definition). What the corpus judges is the compiled evaluator `eval/bsvs`: code
the Lean compiler emitted from those definitions, with each `@[csimp]` substitution applied to the code
compiled after its lemma (a csimp lemma rewrites only the calls compiled after it, which is why
`Machine.lean`, `Numbers.lean` and `Tx.lean` restate the definitions that call a replaced function; two
definitions compiled before the lemmas keep the definitions' folds, being bounded: `Numbers.readNum`, at most
5 bytes, and `deserializeInt64`, which at v1.2.3 `6504a3af` decodes at most nine bytes and
refuses longer operands before assembling a magnitude). The previous pin's reading is retained in a withheld history module at the maintainers' request (group `d8-node-team-discretion`). The spec's lemmas are 36 at this re-cut, `[X]` by `grep -c
'^@\[csimp\]'` over the tracked `.lean` files: 1 in `Eval.lean` (#50), 6 in `Machine.lean` and 7 in
`Numbers.lean` (#45's; the number leaves reworked by #53), 8 in `Tx.lean` (the Lean side's ask 4 (a) and #48's hex
loop), 3 in `Ops/Arith.lean`, 4 in `Ops/Bitwise.lean`, 5 in `Ops/Expansion.lean` and 2 in `Ops/Stack.lean`
(#48's depth-test copies); Lean's core carries its own (`List.length`'s, `Init/Data/List/Basic.lean:2420` in
the pinned toolchain). **Since #50 the compiled `Eval.verifyScript` is `Eval.verifyScriptI`**, the array
machine (`@[csimp] Eval.verifyScript_eq_I`, `lean/BsvScript/Eval.lean`), which the kernel-checked
`verifyScript_eq_I` ties to the definition (through `Impl.verifyScriptA_eq` and `Eval.opExecI_refines`). What
the lemmas reach: 10 shape the script verdict (`verifyScript_eq_I`, the parser's and the push-only test's,
Numbers' seven), 8 the transaction and hex readers (Tx's), 16 act only on the list machine the binary still
runs (the mempool gate's P2SH standardness run and `--trace`: Machine's loop and script lemmas and #48's
fourteen copies), and 2 on the flow leg's list-machine row alone (`Machine.phase2_eq_fast`,
`verifyScript_eq_fast`). The Lean compiler emits C; a C compiler and linker build it against the Lean runtime. The v1.2.3 change preserves the
kernel-checked `Ops.Expansion.substr_eq_fast`, `left_eq_fast` and `right_eq_fast` equalities and the
array-machine `Impl.Expansion.substrI_abs`, `leftI_abs`, `rightI_abs` and `Impl.Splice.splitI_abs`
refinements, with overflow refusal and the `INT32_MAX` split guard on both sides.

The compiled evaluator, the Lean compiler, the C toolchain and the runtime, the compiler's use of the
`@[csimp]` lemmas (the spec's and core's), and the CLI's own code, which no theorem covers (the JSON reader
`Lean.Data.Json`, `lean/Bsvs`'s argument and entry readers, `Run.paramsFor`, `Run.combine` (Q5), `Run.txGate`;
only `Bsvs.Trace` carries theorems), are therefore TRUSTED: a theorem speaks about the definition, and a
corpus verdict speaks about the compiled code. The binary can also abort where the definitions are total
(§4): a resource wall is no verdict (a 750,000-byte literal push killed `eval/bsvs` at 18 GB,
`docs/STATE-OF-THE-WORLD.md`; a stack overflow at 16,000,000 bytes before `e7777f8`,
`docs/records/20260929-020042-ladder48-s1.md`).

The bridge between the two is the kernel-evaluated sample, `lean/BsvScript/Properties/KernelSample.lean`:
25 fixed vectors (arithmetic, stack, flow, splice and the version gate, each with its expected verdict and
error code), each decided by `decide +kernel` on the definitions. The kernel reduces `Eval.verifyScript` as
written; the loop and the push-only test, whose well-founded recursions the kernel does not reduce, go
through fuel copies proven equal to them (`loop_eq_fuel`, `isPushOnly_eq_fuel`); no compiled code and no
`Lean.ofReduceBool` enter. The SAME list is run by the compiled `Eval.verifyScript`, the array machine, in the
tripwire's flow leg (`lean/Test/FlowCheck.lean`, its `kernel sample` line in every record), which exits 1 on
any vector where the compiled verdict is not the kernel's; `vectors_kernel` states the leg's Boolean on the
definitions. So the bridge has two links: the theorem `verifyScript_eq_I` covers the array machine, and the
25 vectors cover the compiler's translation of it. The list machine is outside the sample; the flow leg's
second large-lock row times it on a deep stack. Within one build the flow leg's executable and `eval/bsvs`
link the same compiled module of the evaluator (Lake compiles each module once per build, and the tripwire
builds both); outside the tripwire, the wrapper runs a present binary only when Lake finds it up to date with
the tree's sources, and stamps the verdicts with the commit of those sources, `+dirty` when `lean/` has
uncommitted changes (`eval/bsvs`). The sample is evidence at 25 points, not a proof that the compiler is
correct.

## 4. Termination is definitional, never a theorem (#16, #25; 2026-09-26; re-cut 2026-09-29 after lean #32's second lens, findings C-6, C-8, C-9, and its re-lens, CN-5 and C-9's wording)

Every recursion of the semantics is total by construction: each is structural (on the bytes, a list, a fuel
argument or another `Nat` count: a bit index, a width, a count of items) or carries its `termination_by` measure, and none is `partial` (the only `partial def`s under
`lean/` are the CLI's bsv-rs divergence classifiers, `lean/Bsvs/SelfTest.lean`, and their copies in two
tests, outside the semantics). For instance: the machine's loop is `termination_by s => s.length` over the
remaining bytes (the loop's pattern takes the opcode byte, `op :: rest`, as `GetOp` does, `script.h:173`, and
`getOpTail` a further `k ≥ 0`); the serializer's walk `scriptCodeWalk` and the sigop count
`sigOpCountPreGenesis` likewise; `FindAndDelete` carries fuel (`script.length + 1`, with fuel-irrelevance
theorems); the multisig loop decreases the key count (`termination_by … nKeys`); #50's machine loop, the
hashes' block and round loops and the number codec's limb and halving functions (#45, #53) are well-founded
on the remaining input or a counter. The semantics has no `LSHIFT`/`RSHIFT` chunk loop: the reference shifts
in steps of `INT32_MAX` bits (`interpreter.cpp:1141–1149` for `OP_LSHIFT`, `1175–1183` for `OP_RSHIFT`), and the
spec states one shift by `n`
(`Ops/Bitwise.lean`). Lean accepts each definition only with its measure, so "`run` terminates under the
parameters" (#16's deliverable, the tuple's row 4) is a fact the kernel checks at the definition and NOT a
theorem of the list: no `theorem` states it, none will (#25's scope, item (2)), and a page that calls
termination a theorem is wrong. The compiled binary can still abort on a resource wall (§3).

## 6. What is not claimed (#33, 2026-10-06; the list in full is `docs/WRITE-UP.md` §5)

1. **The transaction-level gate outside the CLI's verdict**: `TxRules`, `TxPolicy` and `Standard` are modeled and
   `Run.txGate` applies them before the scripts, but that gate is CLI code no theorem covers (§3), and the finality and
   sequence-lock rules are outside the scope (`docs/DESIGN.md` §5.5; the class `tx.finality.out-of-scope`).
2. **Block-level consensus**: `docs/BLOCK-CONSENSUS.md` states the boundary and specifies no block rule.
3. **The node's verdicts beyond its recorded runs**: every claim is scoped to the corpus, implementation and commit named in its retained manifest; no agreement is inferred for other inputs or builds.
4. The reading of record at v1.2.3 is a defined refusal ([SRC] `int_serialization.h:61–118`, `script_num.cpp:80–91` at `6504a3af`); the previous pin's reading and its measurements are recorded in material withheld from the public edition at the maintainers' request (group `d8-node-team-discretion`).

6. **The Teranode and SDK columns are conformance runs at named pins, not endorsements**: a column says what one build
   at one pinned version answered on the named corpora in two flag words, with every disagreement classified, and
   nothing about any other version or about fitness.
7. **Everything §3 lists as outside Lean**: the harness, the engines' verdicts, the node oracle, and the compiled
   evaluator's toolchain.
8. **That the reference is correct** (`NORTH-STAR.md` §6), and **any claim of priority**: `docs/WRITE-UP.md` §9 names
   the public work this was checked against and states a dated observation, not a claim.
