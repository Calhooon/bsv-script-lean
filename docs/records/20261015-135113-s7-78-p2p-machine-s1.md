## The theorems [X]

The issue's announcement, "a transaction the mempool accepts is announced to every peer that has not seen it", is
now proven for each of the machine's three acceptances and under any load, qualified as the pin qualifies it (a peer
that relays transactions, passes its fee filter and loaded filter, and is not known to have it,
`src/net/net.cpp:852-876`):

- queued by the next propagator's pass: `accepted_queued_for_unseen` (a peer's `tx`), `rpcAccepted_queued_for_unseen`,
  `retryOrphan_accepted_queued_for_unseen`;
- flushed: `pending_announced_within`, an id at index `i` of a peer's queue is in the `inv` of that peer's
  `(i / broadcastMax + 1)`-th flush, the flushes following one another, for `broadcastMax` above 0;
- announced, whatever was queued before: `accepted_announced_within`, `rpcAccepted_announced_within`,
  `retryOrphan_announced_within`.

The caps: `frame_valid` (the writer's header for a command the frame carries and a payload within its cap is valid
at the same magic), and decided at the run's caps (a block size of 1000001): `s8_header_read` (step s8's 24 bytes
are the `ping` header of 2 MiB + 1 bytes), `s8_header_oversized` (over its cap and invalid; at exactly 2 MiB within
and valid), `protoconf_cap_boundary`, `getblocktxn_cap_boundary` (27930 and 27931), `blockLike_cap_boundary`
(`block` and `hdrsen` at 1000001 and 1000002), `tx_cap_boundary` (10^9 and 10^9 + 1), `basic_header_max_read` (the
length field 0xffffffff) and `extended_header_read` (an extended header of 2^32 bytes, 44 bytes long, over the cap).
